## 2. THE AI VALUE CHAIN — WHERE EACH STOCK SITS

Understanding *where* a company sits in the AI value chain determines *which* indicators matter most for that stock. The chain flows like this:

**Hyperscaler Capex Budget → Chip Design → Chip Fabrication → Server Assembly → Cloud/Data Center → AI Software/Services → End-User Monetization**

### Your holdings mapped:

```
CAPEX DECISION (the master signal)
    │
    ├── CHIP DESIGN ──────── NVDA (GPU king, 90% AI share)
    │                        AMD (challenger, EPYC + Instinct)
    │                        INTC (CPU + foundry pivot)
    │
    ├── SERVER ASSEMBLY ──── SMCI (AI rack-scale solutions)
    │
    ├── CLOUD PLATFORM ───── AMZN (AWS)
    │                        MSFT (Azure)
    │
    ├── AI MONETIZATION ──── META (AI-powered advertising)
    │                        AMZN (AI-powered retail + ads)
    │                        BIDU (China AI/search)
    │                        AAPL (on-device AI, services)
    │
    ├── GOV / DEFENSE AI ─── BBAI (decision intelligence)
    │
    ├── CONNECTIVITY ─────── ASTS (satellite broadband)
    │                        NOK (5G infrastructure)
    │
    └── ADJACENT ─────────── FLY, OPTX, CRM, NOW
```

**Key insight:** When hyperscaler capex goes up, chip designers benefit first, then server assemblers, then cloud platforms. When capex gets *cut*, the pain flows in reverse — chip companies get hit last because of backlog, while cloud margins feel it immediately.

## 3. STOCK-BY-STOCK ANALYSIS

### NVIDIA (NVDA) — $4.2k | Core Growth

**Bull case:** Dominant AI GPU position with 90%+ market share. Trades at ~25x forward earnings — cheaper than AMD and Intel despite far superior margins. $275B backlog for 2026. Rubin architecture extends technology lead. Every dollar of hyperscaler capex that goes to AI chips, NVIDIA gets 90 cents.

**Bear case:** Gross margins peaked at 78.4% and are guided to decline to mid-70s. Custom silicon from Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium), and Meta (MTIA) is a growing competitive threat. China export restrictions limit a $10B+ revenue stream. At $4.6T market cap, even excellent execution may already be priced in.

**What should make you sell:**
- Gross margins drop below 70% for two consecutive quarters (signals pricing power erosion from custom silicon competition)
- Hyperscaler capex guidance gets cut (the entire thesis depends on spending continuing)
- A major hyperscaler (Google, Amazon, or Meta) announces it's moving >50% of AI training to custom chips
- Rubin architecture delays pushing shipments past H2 2026

**What should make you buy more:**
- Forward PEG ratio drops below 0.7 (currently near there)
- China export restrictions ease, reopening $10B+ TAM
- Hyperscaler capex guidance gets raised again

**Undervaluation signals:** Forward P/E below 25x with 40%+ earnings growth = PEG well under 1.0. This is arguably already undervalued relative to growth.

### AMD (AMD) — $8.6k | Core Growth (Your largest tech position)

**Bull case:** EPYC is consistently taking server CPU share from Intel. Instinct MI350 GPU is ramping with partnerships from OpenAI, Oracle, and HUMAIN. Helios rack-scale platform targets the largest AI deployments. Diversified revenue (client, gaming, embedded) provides cushion NVIDIA lacks. Analysts expect 62% earnings spike in 2026.

**Bear case:** GPU gross margins at ~55% lag far behind NVIDIA's 75%. MI-series GPUs still haven't proven they can compete at the high end for training workloads. Gaming segment is cyclical. The stock has already run 72% in 2025.

**Undervaluation signals:** Forward P/E ~33x with 62% expected earnings growth = PEG ~0.5, which is attractive. AMD is cheaper than it looks on headline P/E.

### Intel (INTC) — $6.8k | Turnaround Bet

**Bull case:** The foundry story is real — CHIPS Act investment, Nvidia partnership, SoftBank investment totaling ~$15B. Intel 18A process node is competitive. Domestic manufacturing is a geopolitical advantage in the Hormuz era. Stock is up 68% in 2026. New CEO is executing cost cuts. Demand is outpacing supply in some segments.

**Bear case:** Forward P/E near 70x is the most expensive of the three chipmakers despite the weakest fundamentals. Revenue is still shrinking. The foundry business burns cash. Execution risk is massive — Intel has disappointed on process node timelines repeatedly. The stock is priced for hope, not delivery.

**What should make you sell:**
- Intel 18A production yields fall below 50% (foundry thesis breaks)
- Two consecutive quarters of revenue decline after the current stabilization
- CHIPS Act funding gets clawed back or delayed by political changes
- Forward P/E exceeds 80x without corresponding revenue acceleration (means the turnaround narrative is getting priced beyond any realistic outcome)

**What should make you buy more:**
- First major foundry contract win (a Qualcomm, NVIDIA, or Apple production deal)
- Revenue inflects to consistent growth with margins expanding
- P/E compresses below 40x through earnings improvement rather than stock decline

**Undervaluation signals:** Intel is NOT undervalued on traditional metrics right now. The investment thesis is entirely about option value on the foundry business. This is a binary bet, not a value play.

**Honest assessment:** At $6.8k (15% of tech), this is a large position for a turnaround bet. Consider sizing it relative to conviction — if the foundry story breaks, this could give back 40%+ fast.

## 4. SELL SIGNAL FRAMEWORK

### Universal Sell Signals (Apply to all AI/tech stocks)

**1. Hyperscaler Capex Guidance Gets Cut**
This is the macro kill switch. If Microsoft, Amazon, Google, or Meta announce capex reductions, sell chip stocks first (NVDA, AMD), then infrastructure (SMCI), then platform (AMZN, META, MSFT). The entire AI trade depends on continued spending.

**Data source:** Quarterly earnings calls (MSFT Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, AMZN Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, GOOGL Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, META Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct)
**Update frequency:** Quarterly with interim guidance updates

**2. AI Revenue Monetization Rate Stalls**
The gap between capex spending (~$600B+) and AI-specific revenue (~$100-150B) is the biggest risk to the cycle. If this gap stops closing, the market will start pricing in an AI "dot-com" style correction.

Watch: Cloud revenue growth rates (AWS, Azure), AI-specific product revenue (Copilot, Bedrock, Advantage+), enterprise AI adoption surveys.

**3. Interest Rates Rise / Fed Turns Hawkish**
Higher rates compress growth multiples. A 10Y Treasury above 5% would put significant pressure on all growth stock valuations. This would hit your highest-multiple stocks hardest (INTC paradoxically, then ASTS, BBAI, and AMD).

**4. Hormuz Crisis Escalates Further**
If the Strait stays closed beyond mid-2026 and helium shortages actually impact TSMC production, chip costs spike and the AI buildout timeline stretches. This is a sell signal for the *pace* of the AI cycle, not the thesis itself.

**5. Technical: Death Cross on Major Indices**
If QQQ or SMH form a death cross (50-day SMA crosses below 200-day SMA), it signals a broader tech bear market. In a tech bear, even fundamentally strong stocks get sold. Reduce exposure across the board and raise cash.

## 5. UNDERVALUATION INDICATORS

For each stock, the dashboard should compute and display:

### Primary Metrics

**1. Forward PEG Ratio** = Forward P/E ÷ Expected EPS Growth Rate
- Below 1.0 = potentially undervalued relative to growth
- Below 0.7 = strong value signal
- Above 2.0 = likely overvalued
- Most useful for: NVDA, AMD, META, AMZN

**2. Free Cash Flow Yield** = FCF / Market Cap
- Above 5% = attractive
- Above 8% = strong buy territory
- Below 2% = expensive
- Most useful for: AMZN (normalize for capex cycle), META, AAPL, MSFT
- Not useful for: pre-revenue companies (ASTS, BBAI)

**3. EV/EBITDA vs Sector Average**
- Compare each stock to its subsector peers
- Below sector average = relatively cheap
- Useful for: All profitable companies

**4. Price vs. Analyst Consensus Target**
- Trading 20%+ below median target = street thinks it's cheap
- Trading above highest target = fully priced
- Data source: Yahoo Finance, Refinitiv consensus

**5. Forward P/E vs. Own 5-Year Average**
- Below average = cheap relative to its own history
- Useful for: Mature companies (AAPL, MSFT, AMZN, META)
- Not useful for: Turnarounds (INTC) or pre-revenue (ASTS)

### Secondary / Confirmation Signals

**6. Earnings Revision Momentum**
- Are analyst EPS estimates being revised up or down over 30/60/90 days?
- Rising estimates + flat stock = pressure building for upside move
- Data source: Zacks Earnings Estimates, Yahoo Finance

**7. Insider Buying/Selling**
- Net insider buying (open market purchases, not option exercises) = strong confidence signal
- Heavy insider selling = caution flag
- Data source: SEC Form 4 filings, OpenInsider

**8. Short Interest Ratio**
- High short interest in fundamentally strong stock = squeeze potential
- Rapidly rising short interest = market is betting against the story
- Data source: FINRA short interest reports

**9. Revenue Surprise Trend**
- 4+ consecutive quarterly beats = systematic underestimation by street
- Misses after a string of beats = momentum reversal warning

## 6. GEOPOLITICAL & MACRO INDICATORS FOR AI STOCKS

### Hormuz Impact Matrix

The Strait of Hormuz crisis affects AI stocks through three channels:

**Channel 1: Energy Costs → Data Center OpEx**
Higher energy costs hit cloud providers (AMZN, MSFT) and chip fabs (INTC).
Indicator: Natural gas and electricity futures prices.

**Channel 2: Helium Supply → Chip Manufacturing**
Qatar supplies ~1/3 of global helium. Helium is critical for semiconductor fabrication cooling.
Helium prices have doubled since the war began. If TSMC faces helium shortages, chip costs rise for NVDA and AMD (fabless) and directly impact INTC (has own fabs but less helium-dependent processes in US).
Indicator: Helium spot prices, TSMC monthly revenue reports.

**Channel 3: Shipping Disruption → Supply Chain Delays**
Rerouting around Cape of Good Hope adds ~19 days transit time for semiconductor equipment and components.
Indicator: Baltic Dry Index, shipping insurance rates, semiconductor equipment delivery lead times.
